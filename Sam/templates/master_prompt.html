Master Prompt
This guide helps investigators interpret OSINT/SOCMINT findings using external AI tools.
📋 How to Use This with AI Tools

Follow these steps to get a structured AI-assisted analysis of your OSINT findings. This workflow works with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any capable AI assistant.

1
Run scans in the portal

Complete your investigation using the Main Dashboard, Infrastructure Intel, Image Intel, or any other module. Allow all scans to finish before proceeding.

2
Export or capture your results

Use the Download PDF Report button on any results page to export findings as a structured PDF. Alternatively, take screenshots of the results cards, or use the JSON/CSV download for raw data.

3
Open an external AI platform

Navigate to one of the supported AI platforms below. Ensure you are using a model capable of document/image analysis for best results.

ChatGPT (GPT-4o) Claude (claude.ai) Gemini Advanced Copilot
4
Copy and paste the Master Prompt

Use the Copy Prompt button below to copy the investigation analysis prompt. Paste it into the AI chat window as the first message to establish the investigator role and output format.

5
Upload screenshots or paste results

In the same conversation, upload the PDF report or screenshots of your scan results. If using a text-only model, paste the relevant findings directly into the prompt at the marked location.

6
Review and document the AI analysis

Ask the AI to analyse the findings. Review the output carefully. All AI-generated conclusions must be independently verified by a qualified investigator before use in any legal or operational context.

⚠ Operational Security Advisory: Do NOT upload classified, sensitive case numbers, victim details, or personally identifiable information (PII) to external AI platforms. Use anonymised or redacted data where possible. All AI outputs are advisory only.
📝 Investigation Analysis Master Prompt
Click Copy Prompt above, then paste into your AI platform. Add your scan results at the bottom of the prompt where indicated.
I am a SOCMINT/OSINT analyst in a law enforcement agency. I am uploading [screenshot / file / video frame] from the tool [tool name, e.g., Maltego / Shodan / theHarvester / whois] where I queried [what you queried, e.g., a domain name / email / IP address / URL]. You act as a senior OSINT/SOCMINT analyst for a law-enforcement agency. Explain the results in a way that a non-technical investigator can understand, and to turn the output into practical investigative leads. Rules: - - Do not assume facts that are not supported by the data. - Keep it precise, structured, and easy to understand. - Explain jargon as if the reader is not from networking, cyber, or OSINT.Not too naive, bland or over technical. - Focus on investigation, attribution support, and evidence enrichment. Please analyse the results and provide a structured report in a document with: . Case summary - Give me a short, report-ready summary in plain English. 1.Plain English explanation of every technical term or field visible in the result 2.Investigative value — what this tells me about the target from a law enforcement perspective. Tell me how this can help identify a person, device, domain, infrastructure, pattern, timeline, location, or association. 3.Lead generation — what follow-up queries, tools, or pivots I should try next. Include what else I should look for in the same dataset or related sources. 4.Counter-operation utility — how this information could support a legally approved cyber operation (e.g., infrastructure mapping, attribution, disruption planning) 5.Red flags or anomalies — anything suspicious or unusual in the results worth flagging 6.Confidence level for each finding (High / Medium / Low) 7. Limitations / caution - Tell me what cannot be concluded from the data. - Mention false positives, ambiguity, or missing context. 8. If the data is technical, provide a non-technical summary that can be shared with non-technical stakeholders.
📌 Reminder: This portal is restricted to authorised Hyderabad City Police and Telangana State Police personnel. AI-generated analysis is a decision-support tool only. All investigative conclusions must be validated by a qualified officer before operational or legal use.
